# HOMEBASE for parents

How to set the house up and live with it. Ten minutes for the first part; the rest when you need it.
(If nobody has put HOMEBASE on the internet for your family yet, start with [`DEPLOYMENT.md`](DEPLOYMENT.md).)

## The idea

There are two sides.

- **The wall** — the big screen. It shows a house. Every room is a picture that is also its news: the missions
  board, the day clock, the piggy bank, the pantry, the photo wall, the calendar. A child who cannot read taps
  their animal, then a room. A bluebird called Tock says what to do, out loud, and says it again if you tap the
  loudspeaker.
- **Your phone** — the grown-ups' side. Words are allowed here. You say yes to finished missions, keep the
  shopping list, and set everything up.

Nothing on the wall needs reading, typing, dragging or sound. Nothing on it punishes: a mission not done simply
is not done, "not today" costs nothing, and stars already earned are never taken for something left undone.

## First ten minutes

1. **On your phone**, open your HOMEBASE address → *I am a grown-up, on my own phone* → *Start a new household*.
   Your e-mail address is only a name to sign in with; HOMEBASE never sends e-mail.
2. **Write down the recovery codes** it shows. They are the way back in if you forget your password.
3. **Add it to your home screen**: Safari's Share button → *Add to Home Screen*. It then opens like an app.
4. **Family → Children → Add.** A nickname, an animal, a rough age. For a baby, switch off "has missions and
   stars of their own": they get a picture in the family and nothing to do.
5. **Family → Grown-ups → you → set a wall PIN.** Four to eight digits. It opens the grown-ups' window on the
   wall for a couple of minutes at a time.
6. **Invite the other parent**: *Family → Grown-ups → Make an invitation*. Show them the square to scan, or
   send the link yourself. A grandparent or sitter can be invited as a *helper*: they can say yes to missions
   and keep the list, and cannot change the plan.
7. **Pair the wall.** On the display, open your address and choose *This is the screen on the wall*: it shows a
   code. On your phone: *Family → Screens*, type or scan it, name it "Kitchen wall". A moment later the display
   is the house. (The display is never given a password. You can switch it off from your phone at any time.)

The first time a screen shows the house, Tock shows a newcomer round: your picture, a room, the loudspeaker. A
tap anywhere ends it — and does whatever was tapped. Tap Tock at the front of the house, with nobody chosen, to
see it again.

## Giving the house something to do

**Plan → Missions → New mission.** Pick the picture first — it is what your child will know the mission by —
then the words, who it is for, which days, and how many stars. Two choices matter:

- *A grown-up says yes* (the default) or *stars straight away*. Start with the first; you will see finished
  missions under **Today → Waiting for you** and answer with one tap.
- *With a grown-up*, for anything with water, heat, steps or sharp things. HOMEBASE asks you to look again if a
  mission's words sound like one of those. It is your decision; it only asks.

**Plan → Routines** are steps in order — a morning, a bedtime. A new routine starts from the usual steps for its
kind; reorder with the arrows. On the wall a child sees *now*, *next*, and beads for how far along they are. No
countdown is ever shown to a child. Routines earn no stars unless you decide otherwise (*Family → Stars and
missions*): getting dressed is part of the day, not a job.

**Plan → Rewards** are things to save for: choosing the story, a family game, an outing. Never food, rest,
comfort or time with you. When you set a price, HOMEBASE tells you roughly how many weeks that is at the
present missions, so nothing is out of reach by accident.

## An ordinary day

- A child taps their animal, opens the missions board, taps a picture to see what it means, does it — away from
  the screen — and taps the big tick. It shows as sent. On your phone it appears under *Waiting for you*:
  **Yes**, or **Not yet** with kind words ("Nearly there — one more look"). Nothing is lost by "not yet". When you
  say yes, Tock tells the child at the wall, with a few sparkles.
- The board shows a few missions at a time (*Family → Stars and missions* says how many). A finished one moves
  to the tray along the bottom with its tick, so the day's work stays in sight.
- You can also do all of this at the wall: tap the little window under the roof, choose your picture, enter
  your PIN.
- Stars go in the piggy bank as pictures in rows of five, so a child can see "how many" without numbers. In the
  bank a child can ask for a reward; the stars are set aside until you answer. **Yes** → later **We did it**
  when it has happened. **Not now** gives the stars straight back.
- **Give a star** (Today, beside each child) is for the things no mission covers.
- The shelf at the top of the bank holds a child's **keepsakes**: small badges for things that add up over time
  — a first star, five kind things, five whole bedtimes, asking for help. They are never for days in a row or
  for beating anybody, they buy nothing, and they are never taken away.

## The shopping list

*To buy* on your phone. Type `2 milk, eggs` and press return; paste a whole note and each line becomes a thing.
Tick things off in the shop; both phones and the wall keep in step within a few seconds. **Share** hands the
list to Messages, Notes or anything else as plain text.

On the wall, the pantry shows the list as pictures. A child can ask for something by tapping its picture; it
waits for you. Nothing is ever bought, and no shop is ever opened.

**Siri:** *Family → Siri and Shortcuts* walks you through a two-minute Shortcut so that "Hey Siri, add to
shopping list" works. ([More](integrations/SHORTCUTS.md).)

## The calendar and photos

Your real calendar stays in the display's own **Calendar Mode** — it is better at being a calendar than
HOMEBASE would be, and it keeps working if HOMEBASE is off. Use a *shared family calendar* there, so work and
medical appointments never appear on a wall. In HOMEBASE, *Family → Kinds of day* lets you put up to three
pictures on a day for the children: school, a visitor, a birthday. That is all the children's calendar shows.

For photos, the display's own screen saver with Google Photos is the first choice. HOMEBASE's own slideshow
(*Family → Photos*) is there if you prefer: your phone shrinks each photo before sending it, which also removes
where and when it was taken. *Hide the photos for now* is for when visitors come.

## Sound, calm and night

*Family → The wall*: whether Tock speaks and how fast; little sounds; **calm mode** (nothing moves);
**stronger contrast**; **quiet hours**, when the wall dims, is silent, and rests.

*Family → Sky and weather*: give a rough position (a ZIP code is looked up on your phone and not sent
anywhere) and the wall's sun and moon follow the real ones; switch the weather on and it rains on the wall
when it rains outside — and a small sign hanging from the roof shows what to put on before going out: an
umbrella, a coat, a sun hat.

If the display cannot draw the house's gentle movement smoothly, the wall notices within a few seconds and
stops the clouds and smoke by itself; everything still works. Calm mode stops all movement whenever you choose.

## The planning helper (optional)

*Family → Planning helper*. Off unless whoever runs your HOMEBASE has given it a key for Anthropic or OpenAI
**and** you switch it on. It can draft a routine, look back over a week, tidy the shopping list or explain how
long a reward takes to save for. It only ever makes a draft for you to read and tick; it cannot give or take
stars, and you choose what it may be told (never names, photos, notes or the calendar) and how much it may
spend. "How long until…?" at the top of that page is worked out by HOMEBASE itself and works with the helper
off. ([More](integrations/AI.md).)

## Going away

*Family → Stars and missions → Holiday mode*: between two dates nothing is handed out and nothing counts as
missed.

## Looking back

*History* shows each child's weeks side by side with their own earlier weeks — how many missions out of how
many, where a hand was asked for, stars in and out. They are counts, not a judgement, and children are never
compared with each other. At the bottom of *History*, **Moments worth keeping** is for your own notes — the day
the shoes went on without help — with a date, a picture and whose it was. Only grown-ups see them and nothing
is counted. *Family → Records* says exactly what is kept and for how long, lets you switch the look-back off,
download everything, or delete a child's records or the whole household for good.

## When something goes wrong

| What you see | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A small cloud on the wall: "Out of touch" | The Wi-Fi or the server cannot be reached | Nothing. Taps are kept and sent when it returns; nothing is doubled |
| The wall shows a code again | It was switched off from a phone, or its browser's storage was cleared | Pair it again (step 7). Nothing is lost |
| "Up to date" never appears on your phone | No signal | It keeps your changes on the phone and sends them later |
| You forgot your password | — | *I forgot my password* with a recovery code; or the household's owner makes you a reset code in *Family → Grown-ups* |
| The owner forgot theirs and lost the codes | — | Whoever runs the server can make a reset code ([`DEPLOYMENT.md`](DEPLOYMENT.md) §9) |
| A mission was marked done by mistake | — | *Today* → tap it → *Take it back*. The stars are removed with a line in the record that says why |
| A child's stars look wrong | — | *Family → Children →* the child → *The record of stars* shows every line; *Put a mistake right* adds a correcting line |
| A tablet is lost | — | *Family → Screens → switch it off*. From that moment the server tells it nothing. Its screen forgets the family the next time it asks: at once if anyone touches it, within half a minute otherwise by day, within a quarter of an hour at night |

## What HOMEBASE will not do

Show an advertisement. Send anything about your family to anybody. Listen. Open a shop or buy something. Rank
your children. Take stars away for a bad day. Keep a child at the screen: every job on it happens somewhere
else in the house.
